Fanfiction is where a lot of people meet EPUB for the first time. Archive of
Our Own puts a Download button on every work, the file lands in your downloads
folder, and from that moment the archive's job is done — reading it, putting a
cover on it, keeping fifty of them organised, getting them onto a Kindle: all
of that is yours. The tools on this page cover that whole job, in the browser,
free, with no account. The drop zone above is the actual reader — a downloaded
fic opens in it right now.

Start at the download button, because the first choice is already there. AO3's
software is open source, and [its configuration](https://github.com/otwcode/otwarchive)
lists exactly five download formats per work: AZW3, EPUB, MOBI, PDF and HTML.
The answer for almost everyone is EPUB. It reflows to whatever screen it lands
on, every tool below works with it, and it is the format the rest of your
reading life — phone book apps, e-readers, this page — already speaks. AZW3
and MOBI exist for Kindles, and current Kindles accept EPUB anyway through
Amazon's own [Send to Kindle route](/blog/send-epub-to-kindle), so the Kindle
formats are only worth taking for an older device. PDF freezes the layout,
which is right for printing and wrong for reading on a phone.

Reading needs no install. The [browser reader](/epub-reader) above opens an
EPUB on any machine with a browser — a locked-down work laptop, a library
computer, a borrowed tablet — and nothing is uploaded; the book is read
straight from your device. Phones do it natively too: built-in book apps open
EPUB out of the box.

Now the part the archive genuinely leaves to you. An AO3 download is built
from templates that open the book with a text preface — title, author, tags,
the summary — and include no cover image at all. That is the file as designed,
not damage. But it means a library of downloaded fics is a grid of blank
tiles, and the author field carries an archive username that may sort oddly
next to published books. Both are five-minute fixes: the
[metadata editor](/epub-metadata-editor) sets the title and author you want to
shelve the file under, and the [cover editor](/epub-cover-editor) puts an
image on the front — your own, or something simple that makes the spine
recognisable at a glance.

Series are the other library problem. A five-work series downloads as five
files, and five files is how part three goes missing two phone migrations
later. [Merge](/merge-epub) binds them in reading order into one EPUB with a
table of contents naming every part. One object, one shelf entry, nothing to
lose. And if your folder of downloads dates back far enough to be full of
`.mobi` files from the years before EPUB won, the [MOBI converter](/mobi-to-epub)
brings them forward — that format is retired and the sooner a library is out
of it, the better.

Two honest limits. First: nothing here downloads anything from any site — you
bring files you already have. That matters because not every platform offers
a download button. Wattpad is the big example: no export, and the third-party
downloaders that promise one work by scraping, against the platform's terms.
This page will not link one; the honest answer is to read Wattpad stories in
Wattpad's own app. Second: fanworks are given away by their authors, free, on
the archive they chose. Downloads are for your own shelf — re-posting someone
else's work elsewhere is not curation, it is taking.

Everything here costs nothing and asks for no sign-up. The shelf below is the
whole workflow, in the order the job actually happens.
