Tap a downloaded EPUB on an iPhone and — unlike on Windows or Android —
something sensible usually happens: the phone offers the book to Apple
Books, which ships preinstalled and reads the format natively. If all you
want is to read the book you just downloaded, and Books is on your phone,
that is the answer, and this page won't pretend otherwise: open the file,
choose Books, read.

The searches that bring people here are usually about the other cases —
the ones where the built-in answer stops being the right one.

## When Books isn't the right home for the book

Books is a library, not a viewer. Everything opened in it is shelved on
that device, and with iCloud syncing on, the book follows the Apple ID to
every other device signed into it. That is exactly what you want for books
you own — and exactly wrong in a handful of situations:

It isn't your phone. Reading one chapter on a borrowed or family device
shouldn't leave the book permanently shelved in someone else's library.

You don't want this file in your library at all. A manuscript someone sent
for a quick opinion, a draft of your own, a work document that happens to
be an EPUB — opening it in Books files it; closing a browser tab forgets
it.

Books isn't there. It can be deleted like most built-in apps, and managed
school or work iPhones can hide or restrict it — often alongside the App
Store itself, so "just reinstall it" needs an administrator, and a phone
with no Apple ID signed in can't reinstall it at all.

## The no-install route

The reader at the top of this page runs in Safari — or whichever browser
you use. An EPUB is [a zip of web pages](/blog/what-is-an-epub-file)
wearing a different extension, so the browser already knows how to display
the contents; the page unpacks the container and hands the text to the
rendering engine that was there all along. Tap the zone and the Files
picker opens — Safari puts downloads in the Downloads folder, in iCloud
Drive or On My iPhone depending on your settings, and attachments saved
from Mail or Messages land in Files too. The book opens paged: tap the
left or right edge to turn, and your place is kept while the tab stays
open. Nothing is uploaded — the file is read straight from the phone, and
the [online EPUB reader](/epub-reader) page explains the tool itself in
more detail.

One vocabulary note: people searching for an "iPhone ereader" almost
always mean software, not a device to buy. On iPhone that software is
usually Books; this page is the version of it that needs no library and
no account.

## When Books is the better answer

Honest edges. If you read on your iPhone every day, use Books or another
proper reader app: a real shelf, a reading position that survives days and
reboots rather than a browser tab, and full font and night-mode controls.
This page is the no-trace route, not an argument against the app the phone
already has. Two limits apply everywhere: books bought from commercial
stores — Kindle's, Kobo's, Apple's own — are usually
[DRM-protected](/blog/ebook-drm-explained) and open only in the store's
own app, not in any independent reader, this one included. And if a
DRM-free EPUB refuses to open even in the reader above, the file itself is
probably at fault — run it through the [EPUB validator](/epub-validator)
to see what is broken before blaming the phone.
