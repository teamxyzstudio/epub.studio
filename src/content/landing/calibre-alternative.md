Calibre is the best-known e-book manager there is, and most of what is written
about it is deserved. It is [completely free and open
source](https://calibre-ebook.com/about), it converts dozens of formats, and it
manages whole libraries with metadata, covers and device sync. If you live in
your ebook collection, install it and be happy.

But most people searching for a Calibre alternative are not looking for a
different library manager. They are looking for a way to *skip the
application*: one file needs converting, the work laptop will not allow
installs, or the whole thing feels heavier than the thirty-second job at hand.

That is the case this site is built for. Every tool here runs in the browser —
the drop zone above is the real converter, not a screenshot of one. There is no
download, no admin password, no update nagging, and nothing to uninstall
afterwards. It works the same on a Chromebook, a phone, and the most
locked-down office machine, because a browser tab is all it asks for.

One thing we will not claim: a privacy advantage. Calibre is desktop software —
your books stay on your machine there too. The honest difference is weight, not
privacy. Where the upload-style converter sites do differ from both of us is
exactly that upload; here, as in Calibre, the file never leaves your device.

The other honest difference is philosophy. Calibre is one application with
every feature behind a menu; this site is one page per job. Converting a Kindle
file is [its own tool](/mobi-to-epub), fixing metadata is
[another](/epub-metadata-editor), and each page explains its edge cases in
plain language. The typographic side — choosing the typeface, spacing and
margins your EPUB carries — has a [dedicated editor](/style-studio) with a live
preview, which is the part we care most about.
