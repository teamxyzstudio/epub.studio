The usual advice for turning an EPUB into a PDF on a Mac comes in two
flavours: install a desktop application, or upload the book to a converter
site. The first assumes you can and want to add software; the second means
your file spends time on someone else's server.

This page is the third route. The converter above runs inside Safari or
Chrome on the Mac you are using right now — the EPUB is read from Finder,
re-typeset in the browser's own engine, and handed back as a download.
No admin password, nothing added to Applications, and the book never leaves
the machine. That combination matters most on exactly the Macs where the
first route fails: managed work laptops, shared family machines, and any Mac
where installing one-job software feels like overkill.

What you get is a genuinely typeset PDF, not screenshots of pages: real,
selectable, searchable text, chapters starting on fresh pages, and the trim
size you choose. The [page-size choice](/blog/convert-epub-to-pdf-without-losing-formatting)
is the one decision worth a moment's thought — A4 for printing on the paper
in your printer, 6×9″ for something that reads like a paperback on screen.

Honest edges, briefly. If your EPUB is a scan wrapped in an ebook — page
images rather than text — the PDF will be those images at your chosen size,
because there is no text to typeset. DRM-protected books from stores will not
open at all; no converter can read them. And if what you actually want is a
whole-library workflow on your Mac rather than one conversion,
[Calibre](https://calibre-ebook.com) is free, runs well on macOS, and is the
right tool for that job — this page is for the thirty-second version.
